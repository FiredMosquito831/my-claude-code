## Description

## What changes

The project moves off **PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0** and becomes
**dual-licensed: GNU AGPL-3.0-or-later, or a commercial license by
negotiation.**

PolyForm Noncommercial said something the project does not mean. A company that
reads "you may not use this for commercial purposes" stops there — including
the many users whose only commercial act is routing their own work through the
proxy at their day job. The restriction the author actually wants points the
other way: run it for anything, but do not take the code closed and sell it as
a service. That is exactly the trade the AGPL makes, and section 13 is what
makes it work for a proxy — a hosted, modified version owes its users source.

`LICENSE` keeps the structure it had in 6.39.1: Required Notice block, then a
plain-language preface, then the license text, then the MIT notice for the
inherited upstream code. What changed is the middle, plus a new section 7(b)
term between the AGPL and the MIT block.

## The attribution requirement, as a section 7(b) term

The AGPL does not let a licensor bolt arbitrary conditions on. Section 7(b)
permits exactly one shape of attribution term — "preservation of specified
reasonable legal notices or author attributions" — so that is the shape used,
placed after the license text:

```
# Additional term under AGPL section 7(b)

Section 7(b) of the GNU Affero General Public License version 3 permits an
additional term "requiring preservation of specified reasonable legal notices
or author attributions in that material or in the Appropriate Legal Notices
displayed by works containing it". Under that subsection, one such term applies
to this software and to every covered work based on it:

    You must preserve the plain-text lines beginning with `Required Notice:`
    at the top of this file -- in all copies of the software or of any covered
    work based on it, and in the Appropriate Legal Notices displayed by any
    product or service built on it.

That is the only additional term. It is one of the terms section 7 expressly
allows, and it restricts none of the rights the AGPL grants.
```

The broader ask — *link the repository whenever you write about it* — is
**not** written as a term. Section 7 does not permit it, and a further
restriction is one a recipient may simply strip out; stating it as binding
would have been both unenforceable and misleading. It stays in the preface as a
request, and a test asserts the term body contains no "link"/"mention"/"write
about" language.

## Packaging

`uv build --wheel --offline` on this branch:

```
Metadata-Version: 2.5
License-Expression: AGPL-3.0-or-later AND MIT
License-File: COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md
License-File: LICENSE

my_claude_code-6.39.2.dist-info/licenses/LICENSE
my_claude_code-6.39.2.dist-info/licenses/COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md
```

The PEP 639 expression is used rather than `license = { file = "LICENSE" }`,
which hatchling renders as a deprecated `License:` header with the entire 39 KB
document inlined into METADATA. Trove `License ::` classifiers are deliberately
absent — PEP 639 forbids pairing them with an expression and PyPI rejects
distributions carrying both. The commercial option has no SPDX identifier, so
it lives in `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md`, which ships beside `LICENSE`.

## Residual risks

- **This is not legal advice, and nobody with a law degree has read it.** The
  user should have counsel review the dual-licensing setup before relying on
  it — particularly the section 7(b) term and the commercial-license path.
- **Relicensing reach.** The AGPL applies to the author's own work. The
  inherited MIT portions stay MIT and are compatible, but anyone who has
  contributed to this repository under the previous terms has not been asked
  about the change. For a single-author project that is a formality; if there
  are outside contributions in the history, it is not.
- **Previously released versions stay under their old terms.** Anyone who took
  6.39.1 has a PolyForm Noncommercial grant that this commit does not revoke.
- **AGPL is a real deterrent to some corporate users**, more than PolyForm
  Noncommercial was for their internal use. Internal use is unambiguously fine
  under the AGPL, but some legal departments block AGPL dependencies by policy.
  That cost is deliberate and is what the commercial license answers.
- **`Metadata-Version: 2.5`** needs a reasonably current pip to read the
  license fields. Python 3.14 is required by this project and ships pip 25.x,
  and installation itself does not depend on those fields, so the practical
  risk is nil — but it is a newer metadata version than 6.39.1 produced.
- **The wheel tests do not run in CI** (opt-in flag), so a packaging regression
  that drops `license-files` would be caught only by the always-on
  `pyproject.toml` assertion, not by an actual build.
