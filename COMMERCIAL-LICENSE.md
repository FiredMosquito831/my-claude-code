## What the commercial license is for

The AGPL asks for source code in return. If you convey the software, or run a
modified version of it as a network service that other people interact with,
section 13 of the AGPL obliges you to offer those users the complete
corresponding source of your version under the same license.

A commercial license releases you from that obligation. It is the right choice
if you want to:

- offer this software, or a work based on it, as a hosted or managed service;
- route model or API traffic commercially through it as part of a paid product;
- reuse its code, its routing logic, or its algorithms inside a product you
  ship without publishing that product's source under the AGPL;
- distribute it inside a closed-source appliance, gateway, or bundle.

A commercial license is **the only way to use this software outside the terms
of the AGPL.** There is no other exception, standing permission, or informal
allowance.
